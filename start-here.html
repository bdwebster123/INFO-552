Start Here

If college feels like a big question mark, that's normal. This page is the short version of what to do, why it matters, and where to find help.

What does “first-generation” mean?

You are a first-generation college student if neither of your parents finished a four-year college degree. About half of Baltimore City Public Schools students fall in this group. Colleges know first-gen students often have fewer people at home who’ve done college before, so many schools run special programs and advising for first-gen applicants.

Being first-gen is not a problem. It’s a fact about your family’s history with college, and it can help you with admissions and scholarships.

Why this matters for you

Many scholarships, mentoring programs, and advising spots are reserved specifically for first-generation students. Identifying as first-gen on applications keeps you eligible for these.

A short plan

You don’t need to do everything at once. Focus on these first:

  • ✓ Talk to your school counselor and tell them you’re thinking about college.
  • ✓ Sign up for at least one free program on the Programs Near You page.
  • ✓ Find out when the FAFSA opens and put it on your calendar (October 1).
  • ✓ Take a practice SAT or ACT to see where you stand.
  • ✓ Keep a list of every college you’re curious about, even if it feels out of reach.

Both can lead to a four-year degree. Many students start at BCCC and transfer to a four-year college after two years — this can save thousands of dollars. Talk to a counselor or a program like CollegeBound to decide which path fits your goals.

The FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) is the form you fill out to get federal grants, work-study, and loans. Most state aid and many college scholarships also require it. It’s free. You’ll need a parent or guardian’s tax info. See Pay for College for help.
