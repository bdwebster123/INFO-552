Pay for College

College is more affordable than you think. Most West Baltimore students qualify for grants that cover a large part of tuition — you just have to apply.

FAFSA basics

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is the most important form for paying for college. It’s free, and you fill it out once a year.

What FAFSA gets you

  • Pell Grant — up to $7,395 a year (the 2026–2027 maximum) that you don’t pay back.
  • Maryland State Aid — additional grants for in-state students.
  • Federal work-study — a part-time job on campus.
  • Federal student loans — with lower interest rates than private loans.
  • Many college scholarships also require a FAFSA to be on file.

What you’ll need to apply

  • Your Social Security number.
  • A parent or guardian’s Social Security number and most recent tax return.
  • An FSA ID account (free to create at studentaid.gov).
  • About an hour, with a parent or guardian if possible.

Need help with FAFSA?

CollegeBound Foundation completed over 900 FAFSAs with Baltimore students last year. They’ll sit with you and help. See the Programs Near You page.

Scholarships

Scholarships are free money for college. Some come from colleges, some from local groups, some from national organizations. The best strategy is to apply to many small ones, not chase one giant one.

Local

CollegeBound scholarships

Baltimore-specific scholarships through CollegeBound Foundation, often for first-generation students.

State

Maryland scholarships

State-funded scholarships through the Maryland Higher Education Commission. Filed through your FAFSA in most cases.

National

Common Black College App

Apply to multiple HBCUs (Historically Black Colleges and Universities) with one application for a small fee.

Tuition-free options

Yes, college can be free. The most well-known option in Baltimore is the BCCC Mayor’s Scholars Program, which covers tuition at Baltimore City Community College for eligible Baltimore high school graduates.

BCCC Mayor’s Scholars

Covers tuition and required fees for an associate degree if you’re a Baltimore City resident who recently graduated from a Baltimore high school. You’ll still need books, transportation, and a small fee, but tuition is paid.

Heads up about loans

Federal student loans are usually safe and have a fixed interest rate. Private loans from banks are risky — they often have higher interest and fewer protections. Try to cover college with grants and scholarships first, then federal loans, and only private loans as a last resort.
