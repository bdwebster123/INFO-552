Programs Near You

Three free programs serving Baltimore students. Each one does something a little different. You can apply to more than one.

College advising

CollegeBound Foundation

Who it’s for: Students at Baltimore City Public Schools, grades 9–12.

What they do: CollegeBound places advisors in city high schools to help students with applications, the FAFSA, scholarships, and personal essays. They report over 11,000 advising sessions and 900 FAFSA completions in 2024–2025.

How to get involved: Most Baltimore City high schools have a CollegeBound advisor on staff. Ask your school counselor to introduce you, or sign up for one of their summer programs.

Cost: Free.

Pre-college program

Maryland Ascent Program

Who it’s for: First-generation, college-bound juniors and seniors at Baltimore City Public Schools.

What they do: The University of Maryland’s Maryland Ascent Program supports students through the college application process with workshops, campus visits, and admissions guidance.

How to get involved: Applications usually open in the spring of junior year. The Office of Undergraduate Admissions at UMD has full details on eligibility and how to apply.

Cost: Free.

Visit Maryland Ascent →

Tuition-free college

BCCC Mayor’s Scholars Program

Who it’s for: Recent Baltimore City high school graduates who plan to attend Baltimore City Community College.

What they do: The Mayor’s Scholars Program covers tuition and required fees at BCCC, so you can earn an associate degree without paying tuition out of pocket.

How to get involved: Apply to BCCC, fill out the FAFSA, and submit the Mayor’s Scholars application. The deadline is in late summer for fall enrollment.

Cost: Free tuition. You’ll still need books, transportation, and a small program fee.

Visit Mayor’s Scholars →

How to choose

You can apply to more than one of these. Most students start with CollegeBound because it’s the easiest to access — the advisors come to your school. Add Maryland Ascent if you’re a junior or senior who’s thinking about a four-year college. Save Mayor’s Scholars for after high school graduation, when you decide whether community college is the right next step.
