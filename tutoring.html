Tutoring & Academic Support

Free homework help, test prep, and study skill resources. Getting tutoring isn't a sign you're behind — it's a sign you're serious about college.

You’re not behind

Getting tutoring is a sign you’re serious about college.

Every college student uses tutoring at some point. Starting now means you build the habit before you need it. All of the resources below are free.

Homework help

Most Baltimore high schools have after-school tutoring run by teachers. Ask your school counselor for the schedule.

School-based tutoring

Most Baltimore City Public Schools offer after-school tutoring two or three afternoons a week. Ask your counselor for the schedule and which subjects are covered.

Enoch Pratt Free Library

Branches across Baltimore offer free homework help, computer access, and quiet study space. Some locations have live tutors after school.

Khan Academy

Free online videos and practice for almost every high school subject. The math and science sections are especially strong.

SAT and ACT test prep

You don’t need to pay for an expensive prep course. Most students score better just by taking practice tests and learning the format.

Free

Khan Academy + College Board

The official SAT partner. Take a practice test, and the site builds a study plan around the questions you missed.

Free

ACT Academy

The official free prep site for the ACT, including full-length practice tests.

In Baltimore

CollegeBound test prep

CollegeBound advisors can point you to free SAT/ACT prep groups in Baltimore. Ask your advisor at school.

Important: are tests required?

Many colleges are now test-optional, meaning SAT/ACT scores aren’t required. Check each school on your list. If a test is required, plan to take it at least once in spring of junior year so you have time to retake it.

Study skills

College classes move faster than high school. Building study habits now will help a lot. A few that work well:

  • Active recall. Close your notes and try to write down what you remember. Then check what you missed.
  • Spaced practice. Study a topic in shorter sessions over several days, not all at once the night before.
  • Teach what you learned. Explain a topic out loud to a friend or family member. If you can’t explain it simply, you don’t know it well enough yet.
  • Plan your week. Use a planner or your phone calendar. Block specific times for homework, test prep, and breaks.
  • Sleep. Sleep matters more than one extra hour of cramming. Sleep helps your brain hold on to what you studied.

One small change

If you pick one habit from this list, try active recall. It’s the single biggest difference between students who memorize and students who actually learn.
