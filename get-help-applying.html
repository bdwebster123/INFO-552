Get Help Applying

The college application has a few parts: a written form, an essay, recommendation letters, and sometimes test scores. Here’s what each one is and where to get help.

The application, step by step

Most students apply through the Common Application (commonapp.org) or the Coalition Application. Maryland public colleges have their own forms but ask for similar information. Plan to spend a few weekends on this in the fall of your senior year.

Step 1

Make a college list

Aim for 6–10 schools. Mix “reach” schools, “match” schools, and “safety” schools that are very likely to accept you.

Step 2

Create a Common App account

The Common App lets you apply to many schools using one application. It opens August 1 each year.

Step 3

Gather your info

You’ll need your transcript, list of activities, test scores (if you took them), and a parent’s contact info.

Step 4

Write your essay

The personal statement is about 650 words. Tell a story that’s honest and specific to you.

Step 5

Request recommendations

Ask one or two teachers and your school counselor at least four weeks before the deadline.

Step 6

Submit and file FAFSA

Hit submit before the deadline and file your FAFSA on the same day if you can.

The personal essay

It doesn’t have to sound impressive. It has to sound like you.

Admissions officers read thousands of essays. The ones that stand out are specific, honest, and sound like a real person wrote them — not like the writer was trying to sound smart.

What makes a good college essay

Be specific. Don’t write “I learned that hard work pays off.” Write about the exact moment you learned it.

Sound like yourself. If you wouldn’t use a word in real life, don’t put it in the essay.

Have someone read it. A teacher, a counselor, or a CollegeBound advisor — not just family.

Essay topics to consider

  • A challenge you faced and what you learned from it.
  • Something about your background that has shaped how you see the world.
  • A person who has influenced you and why.
  • A time you changed your mind about something important.

Need someone to look at a draft? Your CollegeBound advisor or school counselor can read it. The Programs Near You page lists programs that include essay coaching.

Recommendation letters

Most colleges ask for one or two letters from teachers and one from your school counselor. Choose teachers who know you as a person, not just teachers who gave you A’s.

How to ask for a letter

  • Ask in person if possible, at least four weeks before the first deadline.
  • Give the teacher a list of the colleges, deadlines, and how to submit.
  • Share a short note about what you’re hoping to study and why.
  • Send a thank-you note afterwards.

Awkward but important

If a teacher seems hesitant, ask someone else. A lukewarm letter can hurt more than no letter at all.

SAT, ACT, and test-optional

Many colleges are now test-optional, meaning you can choose whether to send SAT or ACT scores. If you have strong scores, send them. If your scores are lower than the school’s average, you usually don’t have to.

Free test prep is available through Khan Academy (an official SAT partner) and through several Baltimore programs. See Tutoring & Support.
